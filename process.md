# How I Used AI

## Tools and tasks

I used Codex to review an existing draft of this essay. I first asked it to check grammar and wording. I then asked it to identify confusing arguments, repetition, and weak connections between paragraphs. It gave structural feedback and produced several revised versions. I also asked for Chinese translations and comparisons of the original and revised paragraphs so I could check whether the meaning had changed.

Codex also helped draft this process note based on our conversation.

## One suggestion I kept

I kept this AI-written sentence:

 To build a similar workflow, I need to understand how artistic decisions can become rules that a tool can follow.

It connects the Embark Studios example to my learning goals. My draft described their procedural workflow and then said I wanted to build something similar. This sentence explains what I need to learn to make that possible. It fits my interest in turning my experience as an artist into tools.

## One version I rejected

I rejected a full rewrite that focused the essay mainly on learning programming to improve my workflow. Although it was easier to follow, it weakened the questions I wanted to explore: what makes a work my own, and how I can develop a distinctive approach as AI takes on more creative tasks. Improving my workflow is part of my goal, but it does not cover all of those questions.

The rewrite also changed many of my original sentences. I asked Codex to explain what it had added, removed, and changed. Because I wanted to keep my own wording as well as my argument, so I asked it to preserve the original sentences and work through additions and deletions. I allowed small vocabulary corrections, such as changing "scenarios" to "scenes" in the context of 3D work.

The final version includes five AI-written additions, two sentence deletions, and four vocabulary or phrasing changes. I reviewed that version and asked Codex to apply it to README.md.
